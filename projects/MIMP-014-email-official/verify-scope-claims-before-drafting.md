Facts supplied verbally in this project have twice turned out to be wrong, and both would have
gone into a customer document unchallenged:

- The PO was described as April; it is dated **2 March 2026**, and therefore *precedes* the
  Shanghai site visit rather than following it.
- The scope was described as the cyclotron plus equipment from **13 other manufacturers**
  consolidated in Shanghai. That was not true and had to be cut after Debanjan reviewed a
  draft that already contained it.
- A verbal price "agreement" turned out, on questioning, to be a favourable *tone* in
  conversation, not anything the Chairman actually said.

**Why:** these are commercial assertions to a chairman holding a USD 7M order who has just
inspected a competitor. One sentence he does not recognise costs more goodwill than the whole
email builds — and an untrue scope claim in writing is worse than a weak one.

**How to apply:** before drafting, check anything checkable against the PO and the email
archive rather than the brief, and state the discrepancy plainly when the documents disagree.
For claims that exist only in Debanjan's head — scope, lead times, what a supplier will
accept — ask him rather than repeating the summary. Part of [[brb-smch-cyclotron-deal]].
