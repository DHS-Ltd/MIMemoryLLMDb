### Pain points or challenges for our customer to acquire new equipment:

1. Limited awareness: Many hospital owners lack awareness of the latest technological advancements and how these can benefit their facilities. We want to raise this awareness by providing latest innovation information in medical industry.
2. Mismatch Between Needs and Offerings
Over-Specification: Hospital owners often end up with equipment that is too advanced or unnecessary for their specific needs, leading to underutilization.
Lack of Consultation: Limited guidance or advisory services are available to help owners choose the right equipment for their hospitals.
3. Limited Customization for Local Needs
Non-Tailored Solutions: Most equipment is designed for global markets and may not suit the specific needs or constraints of Bangladeshi hospitals.
Environmental Suitability: Factors like power fluctuations, climate, and space constraints in Bangladesh are not always considered in equipment design.
4. Training and Skill Gaps
User Training: High-end equipment often requires specialized training for operators and healthcare providers, which suppliers may not provide adequately.
Maintenance Expertise: There is a lack of skilled personnel locally who can maintain and repair sophisticated equipment, increasing dependency on external technicians.
5. Complex Procurement Process
Regulatory Hurdles: Importing medical equipment involves navigating complex regulations and customs processes, which can be time-consuming and costly.
Lack of Transparency: The procurement process is often plagued by a lack of transparency, with middlemen inflating costs or creating unnecessary delays.
Documentation and Compliance: Hospital owners often struggle with the documentation and compliance requirements for importing medical equipment.
6. High Costs and Budget Constraints
Initial Investment: Medical equipment, especially high-end machines like MRIs, CT scanners, and surgical robots, requires substantial investment. Most hospitals, especially in rural or semi-urban areas, face difficulties affording these.
Operating Costs: The cost of maintaining and operating sophisticated medical equipment adds to the financial strain.
Access to Financing: Limited or restrictive financing options make it challenging for hospitals to secure loans or funding to purchase equipment.
7. Lack of Reliable Suppliers
Limited Trusted Providers: The market is filled with vendors of varying credibility. Hospital owners often find it difficult to identify reliable suppliers who offer quality products and after-sales service.
Substandard Equipment: There’s a risk of being sold subpar or refurbished equipment disguised as new, leading to performance issues.
